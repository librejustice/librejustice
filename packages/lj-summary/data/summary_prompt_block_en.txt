THE HEADER

The decision arrives under a header. [Décision] gives the court, the date and the number. The following lines appear only when the source carries them: [Nom d'usage], the name the decision is known by; [Titrage], a heading under which the court indexed it; [Nature de l'affaire], the nature the registry gave the case; [Articles de la Convention] and [Conclusion], what the European Court of Human Rights held, article by article. The titrage guides the sort: a point it designates is a point the court indexed. Nothing from the header is copied into the summary, except the case name in the opening: each point is written from the decision.

THE SORTING METHOD

A decision settles more questions than a court indexes, and what it indexes can be recognised in its text before any judgement of interest. Five ranks, preceded by a sieve. The order of checking does all the work: apply the sieve FIRST, to every proposition, and whatever falls through it falls, even with all the marks of a higher rank.

The markers below are quoted as a French court writes them. A decision in another language has its own equivalents, recognised the same way.

THE SIEVE: WHAT YOU NEVER WRITE

Whatever its legal interest and even if your list becomes short. It is not a fallback rank.

- the quotation, paraphrase or indirect report of a text: quotation marks, « aux termes de », « il ressort des termes mêmes de », « ces dispositions prévoient que », « l'article X dispose que ». Check for a reprise before cutting: if a later passage takes up one of its words to decide, the quotation is the very statement of the rule applied and it is kept, written with the words of the reprise.
- the ground of the decision under appeal, or the rule attributed to a party, in indirect speech: « l'arrêt retient que », « si les requérants soutiennent que », « c'est à bon droit que M. X soutient que ». When the decision censures it, the ground itself always falls, but the rule that contradicts it is a point: state it the right way round, never by quoting what is censured.
- the rule attributed to another court or to settled law: « il est jugé que », « le principe ci-dessus rappelé », a cited precedent
- the superfluous addition: « en outre », « par ailleurs », « d'ailleurs », « en tout état de cause », the participial or relative clause that describes a named party or records what an authority did not do
- the dated, quantified or named finding of fact that ends in no legal characterisation: « il résulte de l'instruction que », « il ressort des pièces du dossier que », a past tense, an amount, an address. But the same finding that ends on a legal category (« caractérise », « doit être regardé comme », « revêt le caractère de », « constitue une faute détachable ») is not a finding: it is a characterisation, and it is kept.
- the review of what the trial judges assessed in their sovereign discretion: « appréciation souveraine », « sans dénaturation »
- what has as its very content what a party did not argue or produce: « il n'est pas soutenu que », « ils n'allèguent ni, ni »
- the procedural step that does not decide the question raised: an objection to admissibility raised by a party, no need to adjudicate, a tax relief granted, a plea held ineffective because of a point already settled. Two reservations: the rule of admissibility or jurisdiction stated with a generic subject and a cited text remains a point, and the scope of a cassation remains a point when the decision states it as a rule, with the text that carries it.
- the conditional about an act never taken: « n'aurait pu légalement instituer »
- the ground the decision itself deprives of effect: the one it declares erroneous and replaces by substitution, the one erased by the devolutive effect of the appeal, the one on which no head of the operative part depends
- the rule drawn from a clause of a named and dated contract, protocol or agreement
- what is written nowhere and can only be inferred by comparing two dates

A single exception, that of rank 5: a characterisation may follow « il ressort des pièces du dossier » when it takes up the words of a rule already kept and decides a plea expressly designated (« contrairement à ce que soutient le requérant »).

WHAT REMAINS, BY RANK

RANK 1. WHAT THE DECISION CITES TO ITSELF. A proposition taken up by express cross-reference (« comme il a été dit au point 4 ») or repeated word for word for two limbs, two financial years or two joined applications. The decision itself has designated it as what its reasoning established. Write them all.

RANK 2. THE RULE STATED IN THE COURT'S OWN NAME. In the present indicative, without a name, a date or a figure from the case, placed after the citation or quotation of the texts: « il résulte de ces dispositions que », « il en résulte que », « il appartient à l'autorité administrative de », an indefinite subject (« le titulaire d'un permis », « de telles conclusions »), « toutefois » opening an abstract exception. A main verb in the past tense takes it out of the rank, however general the sentence (« le ministre a pris en compte », « le législateur a entendu reconnaître »). Two checks, and both are required.

THE VOICE. Look at who speaks in the sentence. If it refers to the letter of a text, it is a restatement and not a rule, however well turned: it falls through the sieve. A rule drawn from the combination of two cited texts, which can be read in neither of them, is not a restatement (« il résulte de l'ensemble des dispositions citées aux points 2 et 3 que »).

THE REPRISE. A later passage, the immediate continuation of the same sentence, or the exception the decision sets against it at once, must take up one of its words to confront it with the facts, designate it by cross-reference, or draw a head of the operative part from it. If none of its expressions reappears anywhere else in the decision, it is not a point, however abstract. The reprise is also the only thing that saves a restatement: when a later passage takes up a quoted sentence to draw the outcome from it, write it once, with the words of the reprise.

RANK 3. THE SENTENCE THAT PRODUCES THE OPERATIVE PART. The one that ties the rule to the act under review: « en statuant ainsi, la cour d'appel a méconnu les textes susvisés », « méconnaît ces textes la chambre de l'instruction qui », « alors que » opening the proposition of censure, « en a exactement déduit », « sans rechercher, comme il le lui incombait », « il suit de là que », « par suite », « a pu légalement ». Test: remove it, and a head of the operative part falls or a plea is left unanswered.

RANK 4. THE SCOPE GIVEN TO THE TEXT BEYOND ITS LETTER. A condition it does not write, a field of application, what it does not require, a text set aside, or the extent of the review the judge assigns to himself: « n'implique pas », « aucune disposition ne prévoit », « ne concerne que », « s'entend notamment de », « sous le contrôle du juge de l'excès de pouvoir ». A rule born of the combination of two cited texts, which can be read in neither of them, belongs to this rank.

Every negative proposition is decided on two conditions, and both are required. REMOVAL: remove it, and a head of the operative part must lose its ground or a plea be left unanswered. FORM: either its grammatical subject is a text or a provision (« cette disposition n'implique pas »), or it refers the question to another authority or police power in charge of it, or a « donc », « par suite » or « il en résulte » deduces it from a rule the judge has just stated, or it carries its own express ground. If one is missing, it is a plea dismissed, and it falls. And these always fall, even with the form present: the negation lodged as an aside in the sentence that carries another outcome, the one that takes the point before it as settled, the one about facts the decision nowhere finds to exist.

RANK 5. THE CHARACTERISED FACT, on two conditions and no other: it takes up the vocabulary of a rule of ranks 2 to 4, or it alone answers a plea on which the rest of the reasoning depends. « En l'état de ces constatations caractérisant », « a pu, dès lors, légalement », the condition set two lines above and declared met or not met. Write it with the words of the rule, never with the figures and dates of the case.

RANKS 3 AND 5 ARE NOT POINTS

The rule decides the count. A court indexes a rule, not an answer to a plea. Ranks 1, 2 and 4 are the only entry ranks: they alone open a point. Ranks 3 and 5 attach to one of them and do not exist without it.

- a rank 3 sentence that carries to the operative part a rule you kept is its attachment: it does not count on its own, it completes its point.
- a rank 3 sentence attached to no kept rule is the answer to a plea the court does not index. It falls, whatever its force.
- the same at rank 5: a characterisation of fact attaches to the rule whose words it takes up, or it falls.

A single exception, and it is observed: when the decision holds no rule of rank 1, 2 or 4, because it merely applies a text it does not discuss, keep its one decisive ground, with the text that carries it. A decision in this case gives one point, not three.

THE INDEXING HEADING TEST

A court indexes only what it can classify. Before keeping a point, write the heading it would be filed under in a law report: a subject, a sub-subject, then the question of law, each in a few words and without a name or a date from the case (« Urbanisme - Permis de construire - Étendue du contrôle du juge sur l'aspect des constructions »). If you cannot write it, or if the heading you write is that of a point you already keep, it is not a point: it is an answer to a plea, and it falls.

TIE-BREAKS, BEFORE CONCLUDING

- when the decision declares a ground erroneous and substitutes another for it, or censures and then rules by the devolutive effect of the appeal, keep the substituted ground and never the one it abandons.
- when several propositions answer the same plea or the same head of claim, keep only the one another passage takes up by cross-reference or repetition, or the one that carries the final conclusion. The answers that precede it in the same sentence or the same paragraph, including the path through the texts that leads to it, fall.

WHAT YOU WRITE, ONCE THE SORT IS DONE

The points the sort keeps are what the summary contains, one point per sentence, and nothing else. What the sort sets aside stays out, even exact, even grounding the operative part. You write neither the list, nor the ranks, nor the headings, nor the reasoning of the sort: your answer is the summary alone.

If the sort keeps no point because the decision merely applies a text it does not discuss, write its one decisive ground with the text that carries it. Your answer is never empty.

THE REGISTER YOU IMITATE

That of the law report of the court that gave the decision: the sommaire of the Cour de cassation, the analyse of the Conseil d'État. The register is the same on both sides, long and dense sentences that state the rule with all its conditions without cutting it into pieces, and that name the texts it is drawn from. Four real examples, two of each, in French, to be read as a model of register and not of length:

« Le prononcé d'une annulation fondée sur la méconnaissance des dispositions de l'article 63-2 du code de procédure pénale suppose, en application des articles 171 et 802 du même code, la démonstration par le demandeur d'un grief, lequel ne peut être établi, en ce qui concerne l'absence d'avis donné à l'employeur, ou la tardiveté de cet avis, que lorsque ces circonstances ont empêché ou gêné l'exercice du droit à l'assistance d'un avocat. »

« La production en justice de documents couverts par le secret médical ne peut être justifiée que lorsqu'elle est indispensable à l'exercice des droits de la défense et proportionnée au but poursuivi. La cour d'appel, qui a constaté que la production, par une caisse primaire, de tableaux non anonymisés au soutien de sa demande en répétition de l'indu formée à l'encontre d'un professionnel de santé, était indispensable à l'exercice de son droit à la preuve et proportionnée au but poursuivi, en a exactement déduit que ces pièces ne devaient pas être écartées des débats. »

« Le juge administratif est compétent pour statuer, sur déféré du préfet, sur la régularité de la procédure suivie par les commissions administratives prévues par le code électoral pour dresser la liste électorale. Compétence de la juridiction administrative - Révision de la liste électorale - Déféré du préfet. Les commissions administratives prévues au premier alinéa de l'article L.17 du code électoral sont seules compétentes pour dresser la liste électorale des bureaux de vote pour lesquels elles ont été constituées. Cet article n'a pas conféré à la commission administrative chargée d'établir la liste générale des électeurs de la commune un pouvoir hiérarchique lui permettant de modifier ou de rectifier les décisions prises par la commission administrative compétente pour chaque bureau de vote de la commune. »

« Il résulte des dispositions des articles 1er et 29 de la loi du 30 septembre 1986 relative à la liberté de communication, complétée par la loi du 27 novembre 1986, que s'il appartient à la commission nationale de la communication et des libertés de veiller, lorsqu'elle arrête la liste des fréquences pouvant être attribuées, à ce que, eu égard aux déclarations enregistrées, l'impératif d'expression pluraliste des courants d'opinion, fixé par la loi, puisse être respecté, elle ne saurait, en revanche, être tenue de porter sur la liste susmentionnée, la totalité des fréquences inutilisées, dès lors que cette abstention trouve sa justification dans les limites que fixent à la liberté de communication les dispositions précitées, ou résulte de la nécessité de concilier les différents éléments techniques qui concourent à la diffusion de services de radiodiffusion sonore par voie hertzienne terrestre. »

What carries over from these examples, in any language: the whole rule in a single sentence, with its condition, its limit and the text that grounds it; no sentence spent stating the outcome alone; no word of procedural mechanics; no indexing heading.

THE BUDGET, COUNTED BEFORE WRITING

The court writes short. Count about 350 characters per point on the list, plus 85 for the opening sentence: one point makes about 450 characters, two 800, three 1150, four 1500. Do not go over. If you go over, it is never the rule that overflows, it is what surrounds it: the detail of the case, the reminder of the procedure, the repetition of a condition already set, the justification of the outcome. Cut that, never a condition of the rule. A rule with three conditions takes the room of its three conditions; if it does not fit its budget, that is the only case where you exceed it, and only slightly.

THE OUTCOME CLAUSE, ONCE AND NEVER TWICE

When a rule sentence already says what it produces on the case (« la cour d'appel en a exactement déduit que… », « ayant constaté …, elle a pu retenir que… », « l'administration ne pouvait légalement… »), add nothing. The summary stops there.

When none says it, add the outcome. At the end of a rule sentence or as a separate sentence, as the sentence carries it best: the position does not matter. What matters is that it names what a label does not say, and one of the three is enough:

- the ground: « faute d'un tel grief démontré, la demande est rejetée »
- the object: « d'où l'infirmation du jugement qui s'était déclaré incompétent »
- the scope: « condamnation limitée aux seuls préjudices matériels »

ONE of the three, in 50 to 110 characters, never more. It is a clause, not a second summary: never recount what the decision under appeal had held, nor what it had found, nor the detail of what is upheld. If the clause runs past one line, it has stopped being a clause.

« La demande est rejetée », on its own, is not worth the room it takes: that outcome is already served as a badge beside the summary. What is not there is why, on what, how far.

A [Nom d'usage] of more than six words is cut down to the first name of each party (« <A> et autres contre <B> », « Commission contre <État> »), and it follows the conventions of {target_language}: in French, « v. » becomes « c. » and « and Others » « et autres ». The opening then runs up to 140 characters, never beyond.

Then the points of law, in the register above.

DENSITY, the constraint that prevails

A diluted point is worth less than a point set down cleanly: one drowned in case detail, a repetition, a procedural reminder or the justification of the outcome reads as incomplete, even if exact. State the rule, then move to the next.

Every word carries law or facts. Never procedural mechanics without consequence: « statuant publiquement », « après en avoir délibéré », the composition of the bench, the date of the hearing, the reminder that a decision is enforceable. Never repeat in one sentence what another has already said.

NO ANONYMISATION CODE

The decision replaces names with codes, `[X]`, `[U]`, `Mme X...`, `[Localité 8]`, `[Adresse 2]`. They mean nothing to a reader and they never leave the summary, in any form. Name the role: the employee, the wife, the purchaser, the landlord company, the prefect. A concealed place or date that does not carry the rule is left out.
